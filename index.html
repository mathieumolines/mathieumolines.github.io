About

I am an Associate Professor in Public Management at KEDGE Business School. I hold a PhD from Toulouse 1 Capitole University, France.

My research examines how individuals navigate ethical tensions in public organizations. Drawing on social and moral psychology, I study the micro-foundations of behavior in public service: how mechanisms such as moral disengagement and prosocial motivation shape ethical and unethical actions at work.

Research

Ethics and behavior on the frontlines of public service

A central goal of this strand is to understand how organizational environments and everyday interactions, such as leadership, citizen behavior or institutional framing, transform the way individuals interpret and respond to morally complex situations. I am particularly interested in how psychological processes interact with institutional contexts to produce variation in engagement, decision-making and conduct among frontline public servants.

Postcolonial perspectives on public administration

A second strand brings postcolonial perspectives to bear on public administration and street-level bureaucracy. It explores how colonial legacies continue to shape the conditions under which frontline public servants exercise discretion, contributing to a more historically and politically situated understanding of bureaucratic practice.

Ongoing projects

Three projects draw on interviews conducted in La Réunion, a French overseas département shaped by slavery and colonial incorporation, where history still shapes public institutions, work and identities.

Postcolonial discretion in street-level work

Postcolonial Discretion: How Colonial Legacies Shape Street-Level Frontline Work in La Réunion. How does street-level discretion operate where administration meets a society marked by colonial history? Based on 38 interviews with frontline public servants, this article develops the concept of postcolonial discretion and identifies three forms: positional (whose authority is presumed), linguistic (which language an encounter proceeds in) and translational (restoring the assumptions that administrative categories carry). It shows that some conditions of discretion are historically inherited rather than organizationally produced. Under review. Presented at the ENPA conference (Bucharest).

Crossed identity work: belonging and recognition

Who is recognized, and who is seen as belonging? Based on 34 interviews with managers in La Réunion, a former French slave colony that became a département in 1946, this project examines how people use the categories “local” (born on the island) and “zoreil” (from mainland France) to explain differences in belonging and recognition. Locally born managers belong to the island but must establish their legitimacy at work; metropolitan managers are granted legitimacy on arrival but must earn belonging. We call this configuration crossed identity work and interpret the asymmetry as a form of privilege. Working paper.

Career scripts: the detour and the stay

How do civil service rules, by rationing mobility to and from the island, shape the careers of public servants of local and metropolitan origin? Based on 38 interviews with frontline agents, this project identifies two mirror-image career scripts: the detour (leaving in order to return) for island-born agents, and the stay (coming for a while, then leaving) for agents from mainland France. Both converge on staying. Public service motivation emerges after entry, through contact with users, and the salary supplement attracts people without explaining why they stay. Work in progress.

Teaching

I teach team management, leadership, organizational behavior and human resource management at KEDGE Business School (Bordeaux and Marseille), across the Grande École programme (PGE), Master 1 and Master 2 programmes, and continuing education.

Consulting

Alongside my academic work, I conduct consultancy in leadership and psychometric assessment. I work with organizations using evidence-based tools, including Hogan Assessments, to support leadership development and to better understand individual differences in workplace behavior.
